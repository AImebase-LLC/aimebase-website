## The problem
After 16-hour shifts supporting people with disabilities, DSPs in Maine had to read through pages of client goals and document every meal, activity, and moment. Great caregivers were spending their last, most exhausted hour on paperwork instead of rest.

## What we built
AImdoc asks caregivers a few short, guided questions about the shift, tied to each person's goals, then drafts a professional note ready for review and sign-off.

## How it works
The caregiver opens the note for the person they supported that day. AImdoc already knows that person's goals from their plan, so instead of a blank text box, it asks a short set of plain-language questions: how the morning went, what they ate, what happened with each goal.

From those answers, AI drafts a complete note in the agency's format, with each part mapped to the goal it belongs to. That is the only thing the AI does. The caregiver reads the draft, changes anything that isn't right, and signs off. Nothing is submitted without that sign-off.

## Results
AImdoc is in a production pilot with a care provider in Maine. Caregivers answer a handful of questions instead of writing from scratch at the end of the shift, and every note that goes out has been read and approved by the person who worked it.

## What we learned
A blank note field is the hardest part of the job at 11:40 pm. Asking the same short questions in the same order, tied to the same goals, is easier to answer when you're exhausted than any form, and it gives the AI exactly what it needs to write a good note.
